NAVAL EDUCATION AND TRAINING COMMAND
SEVENTH FLEET TRAINING FRAMEWORK - QUICK REVIEW

Status:
Proposed living framework for Second Life roleplay training.

Full Framework Site:
https://7flt.davefl67.dev

Purpose:
Keep training credible, consistent, and sustainable without turning roleplay into classroom work.
Protect in-world roleplay time while keeping standards clear and practical.

What This Framework Is:
- Roleplay-first training model
- Practical structure for baseline knowledge
- Fun-first approach that supports retention and participation

What This Framework Is Not:
- Real-world Navy training simulation
- Heavy pipeline or administrative burden
- A system that blocks social roleplay participation

Initial Orientation ("Boot Camp"):
- Short orientation, not real-world recruit training simulation
- Focus on terminology, ranks, customs/courtesies, roleplay norms, and expectations
- Goal is confidence and familiarity, not stress or screening

Core Model (Split Training):
1. Off-world LMS for foundational knowledge
2. In-world sessions for scenario application and interaction

LMS Design:
- Short self-paced modules (5-10 minutes)
- Open-book, low-stress quizzes with retakes
- Covers terminology, rank structure, rating basics, safety, and RP expectations
- Unlocks specific drills/qualifications (soft gating)
- Never blocks general roleplay or social participation

In-World Training Design:
- 20-45 minute sessions
- Scenario-driven and story-focused
- Minimal lecture time
- Focus on decisions, interaction, and practical application
- Repeatable and optional where practical

Rating "A" Schools:
- Small number of LMS modules
- Limited short in-world practical scenarios
- Goal: confidence and consistency, not grind

Future Optional Tracks:
- "C" Schools: short enrichment or scenario unlocks for advanced RP specialization
- Officer Education: lightweight orientation/enrichment tracks (OCS, ODS, NPS, NWC concepts)
- All optional and driven by member interest and roleplay value

Expected Benefits:
- Shorter, better in-world sessions
- Reduced instructor burnout
- Faster onboarding
- Better baseline knowledge across the fleet
- Improved long-term retention

Rollout (Incremental):
Phase 1 - Foundation: define LMS requirements, pilot modules, no immediate change to current in-world training
Phase 2 - Integration: tie LMS completion to selected activities and convert lecture-heavy sessions into short scenarios
Phase 3 - Expansion: introduce rating-specific "A" Schools and expand LMS only where roleplay value is clear
Phase 4 - Steady State: maintain LMS baseline knowledge, keep in-world sessions short/social, review and adjust over time

Closing:
This is not lowering standards. It puts the right standards in the right environment so training supports roleplay.
